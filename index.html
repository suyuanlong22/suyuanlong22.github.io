Ph.D. Candidate · Machine Learning · Multimodal Data · Scientific Computing

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Energy, Environmental & Chemical Engineering at Washington University in St. Louis, advised by Prof. Jay Turner. My research develops machine-learning and scientific-computing methods for extracting useful information from environmental observations, including audio, microscopy, sensor time series, and geospatial data.

I received my B.E. in Environmental Engineering from Tsinghua University in 2024. I am from Beijing, China.

My current work focuses on multimodal representation learning, audio classification and source separation, computer vision, and spatiotemporal modeling, with applications to urban noise, heat, and particulate pollution.

Research

Semantic audio analysis for urban noise

Developing source-specific urban noise analysis using CLAP-based sound classification, semantic audio decomposition, and long-term acoustic monitoring. Current models achieved 96.2% zero-shot classification accuracy and 99.0% supervised accuracy on a 12-class environmental sound dataset.

Hyperlocal urban temperature modeling

Building LightGBM models that combine sensor observations, meteorology, and Swin-Transformer representations of high-resolution imagery to estimate neighborhood-scale air temperature. Continuous withheld-period RMSE is 0.33°C, with spatial leave-site-out RMSE of 0.34–0.52°C.

Multimodal single-particle classification

Developing automated classification workflows for electron-microscopy particle data using elemental composition and morphology. The current dataset includes 4,302 particles from 21 passive-sampler datasets with 28 engineered predictors.

Dust-event detection from sensor time series

Developed an unsupervised event-detection workflow for long-term particulate-matter data, combining targeted feature engineering with non-negative matrix factorization. Evaluation against reported events produced approximately 84% sensitivity and 89% precision.
