About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Energy, Environmental & Chemical Engineering at Washington University in St. Louis, advised by Prof. Jay Turner. I received my B.E. in Environmental Engineering from Tsinghua University in 2024.

My research lies at the intersection of machine learning, multimodal data, scientific computing, and environmental sensing. I develop computational methods for extracting source-specific and spatially resolved information from audio, microscopy, sensor time series, and geospatial data.

My current work includes semantic urban-noise analysis, hyperlocal temperature modeling, automated single-particle classification, and particulate-matter event detection.

Research

Semantic audio analysis for urban noise
Machine-learning methods for source-specific urban noise exposure using long-term audio recordings, CLAP-based sound classification, and semantic audio decomposition.
Hyperlocal urban temperature modeling
Multimodal modeling of neighborhood-scale air temperature using dense sensor observations, meteorology, high-resolution imagery, Swin Transformer representations, and LightGBM.
Automated single-particle classification
Machine-learning workflows for classifying microscopy-derived particles using elemental composition, morphology, and multimodal particle descriptors.
Particulate-matter event detection
Unsupervised detection and quantification of dust events from long-term sensor time series using feature engineering and non-negative matrix factorization.
