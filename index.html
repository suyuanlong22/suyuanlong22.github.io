About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Energy, Environmental & Chemical Engineering at Washington University in St. Louis, advised by Prof. Jay Turner. I received my B.E. in Environmental Engineering from Tsinghua University in 2024.

I work on machine learning for environmental data, including audio, microscopy, sensor time series, and geospatial data. My current projects cover urban noise, neighborhood-scale temperature, particle classification, and dust-event detection.

I am from Beijing, China.

Research

Urban noise and semantic audio analysis

I use CLAP-based sound classification and audio source separation to identify noise sources in long-term urban recordings and estimate source-specific noise exposure.

Hyperlocal temperature modeling

I combine field measurements, weather data, high-resolution imagery, Swin Transformer features, and LightGBM to model neighborhood-scale air temperature.

Single-particle classification

I build machine-learning workflows for classifying microscopy particles from elemental composition and particle morphology.

Dust-event detection

I use long-term particulate-matter sensor data and non-negative matrix factorization to detect and quantify regional dust events.
